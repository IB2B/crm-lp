# GoHighLevel's Marketing Site and White-Label GHL CRM Resellers: Landing Page Patterns

Research date: 2026-10-08. All live-page descriptions are from fetches made on that date unless noted. Several reseller examples are only *suspected* to be GHL-based (no public "powered by" credit), so each one is labelled confirmed or suspected below.

## 1. How gohighlevel.com structures its homepage and pricing page

### Takeaway
GHL's homepage runs as follows: an AI-first hero with a "Start 14 Day Free Trial" CTA, then animated usage stats, award badges, a five-tab lifecycle feature section (Capture, Nurture, Close, Evangelize, Reactivate), value pillars, community and testimonials, a two-plan pricing preview ($97/$297), integrations, "HighLevel vs X" comparison links, and a final trial CTA. The pricing page opens with the trial signup and has four tiers: $97 Starter, $297 Unlimited, $497 Agency Pro (where SaaS Mode lives), and Enterprise (sales-led, includes the white-label mobile app and HIPAA).

### Inferences
- GHL's own consumer-facing price anchors are $97 and $297. Anyone who googles "GoHighLevel pricing" sees them immediately, so a reseller charging $297 or more for one sub-account invites a direct comparison. The counter-argument is that GHL's $97 plan is an *agency* plan that requires the buyer to set up and run everything themselves. That gap is what the reseller sells.
- The lifecycle tab structure (Capture, Nurture, Close, Evangelize, Reactivate) is a reusable feature taxonomy. Many resellers copy a three-step version: Capture, Nurture, Close.
- GHL uses "HighLevel vs X" comparison pages as an SEO and objection-handling layer, and resellers copy this too (SMBcrm, LeadSpheres).

- Whether the trial needs a card was not verified on the live page.

### Inferences
- **Self-serve pattern** (The Circle, Wealthy CRM, LeadSpheres, SMBcrm): one price ($97–$199), a 14-day trial, a value stack or "X tools replaced" savings table, cancel anytime, data export, and "running in a day or minutes" onboarding.
- **Demo-led vertical pattern** (Aesthetix): niche-specific outcome headline ("Leads to Patients"), niche integrations (EMR/PMS), compliance badge (HIPAA), logo wall, and price behind a click.
- The better pages:
  - lead with one niche pain ("Stop Losing Jobs to Missed Calls") rather than "all-in-one";
  - show a concrete guarantee (SMBcrm's 60-day money-back);
  - offer data migration ("we handle the heavy lifting");
  - publish trust infrastructure (Trust Center, status page);
  - include results disclaimers.
- The weaker pages (AgentPlus) are minimal feature grids with no trust signals, trial or onboarding story. Several pages carry unsourced and inconsistent stats (The Circle, LeadSpheres), which is a credibility risk to avoid.

## 3. White-label positioning pitfalls: disclosure, GHL terms, "just GHL" exposure, and justifying the markup

### Takeaway
None of the resellers I examined names GoHighLevel. GHL's Terms (updated June 2026) allow rebranding with your own name and logo. They forbid using HighLevel marks without written permission, forbid misleading statements and representing yourself as HighLevel, and make the reseller solely liable for its customers, their support and their compliance. Exposure is real: GHL strings in page source, the LeadConnector name in app, telephony and cookie contexts, and public $97/$297 pricing. Resellers therefore justify their price through niche setup, migration, support, guarantees and outcomes rather than through the software itself.

### Cited Findings
**What GHL's Terms of Service say ("Last Updated: June 2026")**
- License to white label: "portions of the Platform may be modified by you, incorporating your name, logo, trademark". The license is "limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable and revocable". — [GHL Terms of Service](https://www.gohighlevel.com/terms-of-service)
- Reseller liability: "you are fully liable to your customers for their access to and use of the Platform"; "you are solely responsible for the resolution of all customer disputes and inquiries". — [GHL Terms of Service](https://www.gohighlevel.com/terms-of-service)
- Trademarks: HighLevel marks "may not be used without advance written permission of HighLevel". — [GHL Terms of Service](https://www.gohighlevel.com/terms-of-service)
- Claims: resellers must not "make any unauthorized representations, warranties or false, misleading or deceptive statements" and are "prohibited from representing yourself as a HighLevel employee". — [GHL Terms of Service](https://www.gohighlevel.com/terms-of-service)
- Messaging and compliance: "You are exclusively responsible for all communications sent using the Platform", including "10DLC registration and use requirements". HIPAA compliance and PHI handling are the user's sole responsibility. — [GHL Terms of Service](https://www.gohighlevel.com/terms-of-service)
- GHL supports only the agency, so the whole client-facing support layer is the reseller's to build. — [ghlops.com](https://ghlops.com/blog/gohighlevel-saas-mode-guide)

- Comparable point solutions give an external anchor: Podium is around $300–$399/mo and Birdeye around $299–$449/mo per location. — [search summary: sonant.ai, servicebusinessacademy.org](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/missed-call-text-back)

### Inferences
**What NOT to say on the ib2b page**
- Do not say "GoHighLevel", "HighLevel" or "LeadConnector", and do not use their logos, in copy, ads or comparison pages without written permission (ToS Section 5).
- Do not claim to have "built", "invented" or "developed proprietary" technology in a way that could count as a "false, misleading or deceptive statement". "Our platform" or "the ib2b CRM" is safer than "our proprietary AI built in-house".
- Do not promise "HIPAA-compliant" unless ib2b is on GHL Enterprise or otherwise has a BAA in place and will take responsibility, since GHL puts PHI handling solely on the reseller.
- Do not promise "texts delivered instantly" or "start texting today". A2P 10DLC registration takes days to weeks.
- Do not publish unsourced or inconsistent stats (The Circle and LeadSpheres show the risk).
- Do not promise a "branded mobile app" unless the white-label app is purchased and approved in the stores. Otherwise say "mobile app for iOS and Android" and disclose in onboarding that the app is "LeadConnector".

**Exposure strategy**
- Assume savvy buyers will find out the platform is GHL. Price on the service layer and say so plainly: setup, migration, niche snapshot, A2P registration handled, local support and training, a guarantee.
- Several practitioners argue for quiet transparency, mentioning the underlying platform in onboarding or FAQ ("built on an enterprise platform used by thousands of agencies") rather than hiding it. This avoids the trust damage reported when clients discover it themselves. Exact wording that names GHL should be checked against ToS Section 5.

### Gaps
- I could not open specific r/gohighlevel threads on disclosure; the views above come via secondary summaries.
- The last ~20% of the GHL ToS was not read (it was truncated at Section 15).
- I found no official GHL brand or trademark guideline document beyond the ToS clause.
- The current official price and tier of the white-label mobile app is unresolved; sources conflict.

## 4. Technical realities affecting landing page claims (LeadConnector app, LC Phone, A2P 10DLC, Twilio)

### Takeaway
Every US sub-account that sends automated SMS must complete A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration in GHL's Trust Center. This brings fees and approval waits, and registration is rejected if the website lacks a privacy policy and terms or if the support email domain doesn't match the business. The default mobile app is the "LeadConnector" app, and telephony and email may show GHL or LeadConnector branding unless configured. Landing page claims about instant SMS, branded apps and compliance must be hedged accordingly.

### Cited Findings
- All major US carriers block unregistered 10DLC A2P traffic; blocking started August 31, 2023. One source says outright blocking has applied since February 2025. — [ghlscaleup.com](https://www.ghlscaleup.com/blog/what-is-a2p-10dlc); [omnionlinestrategies.com](https://omnionlinestrategies.com/blog/how-to-set-up-gohighlevel-a2p-10dlc-sms-compliance)
- Registration is per sub-account under Settings > Phone Numbers > Regulatory Compliance (Trust Center). Brand first, then campaign. On approval, GHL auto-creates a messaging service and links the numbers. — [GHL help: A2P brand registration](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001225526-lc-phone-system-trust-center)
- Brand types: Low Volume Standard (requires an EIN, up to 6,000 SMS segments/day), Standard (high volume), and Sole Proprietor (no EIN). Official fee for Low Volume Standard is $23.95 one-time (including a $3 Fast Track fee, ~3 business days) plus a monthly campaign fee of up to $12. An older 2024 guide cites lower fees, which are likely outdated. — [GHL help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001225526-lc-phone-system-trust-center); [growthable.io](https://growthable.io/gohighlevel-tutorials/phone/exploring-a2p-10dlc-a-comprehensive-guide-to-its-functionality-with-gohighlevel/)
- Non-US businesses (relevant if ib2b clients sit outside the US) can register with a valid US or Canadian address. — [GHL changelog](https://ideas.gohighlevel.com/changelog/trust-center-a2p-registration-for-non-us-countries)
- Approval timelines vary by source:
  - Campaign vetting 3–7 business days (Fiverr vendor).
  - "Two to six weeks", with fees of $22.50–$64 (affiliate article).

### Inferences
- Make "We handle your A2P 10DLC / SMS carrier registration" an explicit onboarding deliverable. It is a real pain point and a markup justifier, and competitors rarely mention it.
- The CRM landing page itself (and each client's site) needs a privacy policy, terms and an SMS opt-in disclosure on any form that collects phone numbers. Otherwise ib2b's own and its clients' A2P campaigns risk rejection.
- Pricing copy should state that SMS, calls, email and AI usage are billed separately or included up to X, as The Circle does with its "Usage Rates" page, to avoid surprise-bill churn.
- Timeline claims should read "SMS live after carrier approval (typically within X business days)", not "text from day one".
