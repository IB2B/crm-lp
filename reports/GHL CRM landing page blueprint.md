# Sell the Setup, Not the Software

A high-converting landing page for ib2b's white-labeled GoHighLevel CRM has to sell a **done-for-you service with clear accountability**. Software access alone won't carry it. "All-in-one CRM with AI" is now table stakes: GoHighLevel's own homepage, every reseller studied, and HubSpot, Zoho, Close, Vendasta and Monday all lead with AI or tool consolidation. GHL's public **$97 and $297 plans** also sit one search away from any buyer ([GHL pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)). The pains SMB buyers report about GHL and its rivals are about setup and cost, not missing features: a steep learning curve, metered "wallet" charges, A2P/SMS registration, email deliverability, and the HubSpot price cliff ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-disadvantages.md); [HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/CRM/Create-competitive-pricing-for-small-companies/m-p/619782/highlight/true)). The page's job is to promise and prove five things: setup included, predictable pricing, a named human team, niche-ready templates, and fast lead response. It should present that in plain, 5th–7th-grade language, mobile-first, with a dual CTA of trial or "book a setup call" and a compliant phone-capture form. Several things must stay off the page: GoHighLevel, HighLevel or LeadConnector marks, unsourced stats, "HIPAA-compliant", "text from day one", and "your own branded app" unless each is true and permitted. A section-by-section build blueprint and the decisions ib2b must make before development close the report.

## The market splits into three templates, and none sells accountability

**GoHighLevel's own site sets the reference pattern that resellers copy.** The homepage runs in this order: an AI-first hero ("The AI-powered business operating system") with a "Start 14 Day Free Trial" CTA, animated usage counters, review-site badges, and a five-tab lifecycle feature section. The tabs are Capture, Nurture, Close, Evangelize and Reactivate, each with an outcome headline such as "Get more leads in the door". After those come value pillars, community and testimonials, a two-plan pricing preview, integrations, "HighLevel vs X" comparison links, and a closing "No obligation, cancel at any time" CTA ([gohighlevel.com](https://www.gohighlevel.com/)). The pricing page has four tiers: Starter $97, Unlimited $297 ("Most Popular"), Agency Pro $497 (where SaaS Mode lives), and a sales-led Enterprise tier that carries the white-label mobile app and HIPAA. The trial auto-bills at the end, there is no money-back guarantee, and the page notes that telecom usage can be billed even during the trial ([GHL pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)). GHL also publishes vertical "playbooks", such as a Medical Spa bundle of snapshot, funnels and workflows at a stated "$997 value". This shows that GHL itself treats niche packaging as the thing that sells ([GHL Medical Spa Playbook](https://www.gohighlevel.com/medical-spa-playbook)).

**White-label resellers fall into two archetypes, and neither names GoHighLevel.** The first is the self-serve SaaS page: one price between $97 and $199, a 14-day trial, a "replace your tool stack" value table, cancel anytime, and onboarding in "a day". Examples are SMBcrm ("starts at $97", 60-day money-back guarantee, "we handle the heavy lifting" migration) ([smbcrm.com](https://smbcrm.com/)) and The Circle (a single $197 tier whose table claims $1,612 of separate tools and "88% savings") ([thecirclecrm.com](https://www.thecirclecrm.com/)). Wealthy CRM ($147, prebuilt home-services pipeline, results disclaimer) ([wealthy-crm.com](https://wealthy-crm.com/)) and LeadSpheres ("Stop Losing Jobs to Missed Calls", no-card trial) ([leadspheres.com](https://leadspheres.com/)) follow the same pattern. The second archetype is the demo-led vertical CRM. Aesthetix CRM hides its price behind "Book a Demo" and sells med spas a "lead-to-patient journey" with EMR/PMS integrations, G2/Capterra badges and a HIPAA label ([aesthetixcrm.com](https://aesthetixcrm.com/)). The weakest example, AgentPlus, is a bare feature grid with no trial, no guarantee and no onboarding story ([agentpluscrm](https://ai.agentpluscrm.com/)). The best pages lead with one niche pain rather than "all-in-one". They state a concrete guarantee, offer migration, and publish trust infrastructure; SMBcrm links a Trust Center and status page. The Circle and LeadSpheres show the opposite risk: their own pages contradict themselves on tools replaced, savings and uptime.

**Mainstream SMB CRMs sell either a free or no-card trial with per-seat tiers, or a flat-price all-in-one.** HubSpot, Pipedrive, Close, Zoho, Bigin and Monday combine free plans or 14–15-day no-card trials, per-seat pricing, G2 badges and a named AI agent. Close sells "Chloe", which calls, qualifies and books ([Close](https://www.close.com/)); Vendasta sells an "AI Workforce" of ten roles ([Vendasta](https://www.vendasta.com/)). Kartra, systeme.io, Keap and Vendasta price flat by contacts or minimums and lean on "replaces X tools" claims. systeme.io is the only one that shows explicit stack math: $245/mo of separate tools against $17 ([systeme.io pricing](https://systeme.io/pricing)). Onboarding is the revealing gap. **Keap requires a paid, unpriced implementation**, Vendasta charges an at-signing fee plus "Setup as a Service", and Monday and Zoho reserve white-glove migration for their top tiers ([Keap pricing](https://keap.com/pricing); [Vendasta pricing](https://www.vendasta.com/pricing/); [monday](https://monday.com/crm/pricing)). No competitor studied combines four things: an omnichannel inbox; funnels, booking, reviews and payments in one product; done-for-you setup included in the price; and a named human team accountable for results. That combination is where ib2b can stand.

| Archetype | Examples | Entry offer | Pricing model | What ib2b should borrow | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platform (reference) | GoHighLevel | 14-day trial, auto-bills | Flat $97/$297/$497 | Lifecycle tab taxonomy, vs-pages | AI-first generic hero, no guarantee |
| Self-serve reseller | SMBcrm, The Circle, Wealthy CRM, LeadSpheres | 14-day trial or "get started" | One flat price $97–$199 | Stack-replacement table, guarantee, migration promise | Unsourced, inconsistent stats |
| Demo-led vertical | Aesthetix CRM | Book a demo | Hidden | Niche outcome headline, niche integrations | Unearned compliance badges |
| Sales CRM | HubSpot, Pipedrive, Close, Zoho/Bigin, Monday | Free plan or no-card trial | Per seat, often with minimums | Named AI jobs, review badges, refund guarantees | Per-seat complexity |
| Flat all-in-one | Keap, Kartra, systeme.io, Vendasta | Trial, free plan or demo | Flat by contacts or minimums | Onboarding call, stack math | Paid or required implementation |

## Buyer pains point to five messaging angles, while "AI all-in-one" alone is saturated

**The complaints are consistent across platforms, and every one of them is about setup or surprise cost.** GHL holds a 4.9 Trustpilot score across about 15,000 reviews. Even so, Trustpilot's summary of its recent negative themes cites onboarding that is "frustrating, slow, or disorganized", along with unexpected financial hurdles ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gohighlevel.com)). A disclosed affiliate catalogues the structural pains. Every tool has its own settings screen. SMS, email and premium workflow actions are metered from an auto-refilling wallet on top of the $97–$497 plans. Deliverability depends on SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Complex support issues take several rounds. Menus move with weekly releases ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-disadvantages.md)). A2P 10DLC is a pain of its own: one vendor estimates **roughly a third of self-submitted registrations are rejected on the first try**, most often because the brand email doesn't match the domain ([Ecosire](https://ecosire.com/de/blog/gohighlevel-a2p-10dlc-compliance-guide); [Signal House](https://signalhouse.io/guide/why-lc-phone-rejects-your-a2p-campaigns/)). HubSpot users complain of a jump from roughly $50 to $1,600/mo with nothing in between ([HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/CRM/Create-competitive-pricing-for-small-companies/m-p/619782/highlight/true)), plus onboarding fees of $1,500–$7,000 ([StartupOwl](https://startupowl.com/reviews/hubspot)). Pipedrive's top G2 complaint is missing features, with marketing sold as add-ons, and Zoho's is its learning curve ([G2 compare](https://www.g2.com/compare/pipedrive-vs-pipeline-crm-vs-zoho-crm); [G2 Zoho vs Pipedrive](https://www.g2.com/compare/zoho-crm-vs-pipedrive)). Agency sources add that **the top reason clients churn from white-labeled platforms is that they never learned to use them**, and that the lowest-churn agencies spend the most time training clients in the first 30 days ([Ecosire guide](https://ecosire.com/blog/gohighlevel-white-label-saas-complete-guide)).

**The outcomes worth leading with are speed-to-lead and recovered missed calls, backed by citable research.** The MIT/InsideSales study, covering 15,000+ leads, found **contact odds 100x higher at 5 minutes than at 30** ([MIT study](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)). The HBR audit of 2,241 firms found an average response time of 42 hours, and firms answering within an hour were nearly 7x more likely to qualify the lead ([AInora summary](https://ainora.lt/blog/lead-response-time-statistics-every-study-2026)). A 411 Locals study of 85 small businesses found **62% of calls went unanswered** ([Alliance Virtual Offices](https://www.alliancevirtualoffices.com/virtual-office-blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/54623)). The popular "85% never call back" and "78% buy from the first responder" figures have murky or no provenance and should stay off the page.

These findings point to five messaging angles, in priority order:

1. **"Never miss a lead again."** Missed-call text-back, instant auto-replies and AI that books appointments around the clock. AI is credible only when tied to a concrete job: competitors have moved from "AI-powered" to named agents doing specific work.
2. **"We set it up for you."** Migration, pipelines, calendars, A2P registration, email domain authentication and training, live in a stated number of days. This answers Keap's paid implementation and Vendasta's at-signing fee.
3. **"One predictable bill."** Flat price, unlimited users, and usage either bundled or published. This counters both GHL's wallet surprises and per-seat pricing with seat minimums at HubSpot, Close and Monday.
4. **"A real team, not a ticket queue."** A named onboarding specialist and a support response-time commitment.
5. **"Built for your industry."** Niche snapshots shown in a demo.

"Replace 10 tools" and "unlimited everything" are saturated and should support these angles, not lead them.

**For Morocco and MENA, WhatsApp replaces SMS as the lead channel.** Nearly all Moroccan internet users use WhatsApp monthly ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1146768/whatsapp-users-in-morocco/)). Penetration is about 90% in the UAE and Kuwait and 80% in KSA ([Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/messaging-in-mena-is-entering-a-new-phase-and-marketers-must-adapt-fast/)). A2P 10DLC is a US-only concern. A French or Arabic page should therefore lead with "WhatsApp inbox + automations + booking", while a US page leads with SMS and missed-call text-back. This is the single biggest copy fork the team has to resolve.

## Pricing should anchor against the tool stack, not against GoHighLevel's $97

**Assume every buyer can find GHL's $97 and $297 prices, and price the service layer openly.** Agencies typically resell white-labeled GHL at $97–$297 per client ([callin.io](https://callin.io/gohighlevel-pricing-explained-simply/)), with some guides citing $197–$497 ([courseplatformsreview](https://www.courseplatformsreview.com/blog/highlevel-saas-mode/)). One-time setup fees of $500–$3,000 are described as standard ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-agency-pricing-guide/)), and done-for-you med spa builds are quoted from $995 to $2,500 ([autoesta](https://autoesta.com/gohighlevel-medspa-setup-service/)). Running SaaS Mode, with automated sub-account creation and rebilling with markup, needs GHL's $497 Agency Pro plan ([GHL pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)). The price should therefore be framed against what the buyer would otherwise assemble: a CRM, email marketing, SMS, a scheduler, a review tool, a website or funnel builder, and invoicing. Point solutions such as Podium (about $300–$399/mo) and Birdeye (about $299–$449/mo per location) are useful external anchors ([sonant.ai](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/missed-call-text-back)). Never invite a comparison with GHL itself.

**Use three tiers with the middle one highlighted, annual billing on by default, and a separate setup card.** Every per-seat CRM reviewed highlights its middle or upper-middle plan, and their annual discounts range from 18% to 65%. Paddle's guidance puts the sensible annual discount at **15–20%**: below that few people switch, and above 30% it reads as an inflated monthly price ([Paddle/ProfitWell](https://learn.profitwell.com/resources/best-discounts-for-saas-companies-with-paddle)). "Two months free" (16.7%) is the common framing, and ProfitWell's Campbell recommends stating savings in currency rather than percent ([Player.fm summary](https://ppacc.player.fm/1BQqQpA)). The done-for-you setup belongs on its own card as a one-time fee, optionally waived on annual plans, which also gives buyers a natural reason to choose annual. A dated, sourced stack-cost comparison should sit beside the pricing. The Circle and systeme.io show the format works ([thecirclecrm.com](https://www.thecirclecrm.com/); [systeme.io pricing](https://systeme.io/pricing)), but The Circle also shows how inconsistent numbers destroy credibility.

**Usage charges must be visible on the page.** SMS runs about $0.0075 per segment plus carrier fees, and email about $0.675 per 1,000. Resellers commonly mark these up 2–4x, and buyers report usage costs that "accumulate faster than expected" ([courseplatformsreview](https://www.courseplatformsreview.com/blog/highlevel-saas-mode/); [thestackinsiders](https://www.thestackinsiders.com/blog/gohighlevel-reddit)). Either include an allowance in each tier ("includes 1,000 SMS/month") or link a Usage Rates page, as The Circle does. That makes "no surprise bills" a claim ib2b can keep, and the risk of absorbing heavy usage becomes something ib2b controls rather than discovers.

**Add a guarantee that is specific and visible.** Close, Kartra, ActiveCampaign and Bigin offer 30-day money-back guarantees ([Close pricing](https://www.close.com/pricing); [Kartra pricing](https://kartra.com/pricing/)), and SMBcrm repeats a 60-day guarantee throughout its page ([smbcrm.com](https://smbcrm.com/)). The only test found is old and non-SaaS: a visible 30-day guarantee lifted sales 21% with a 12% refund rate, but it added nothing on top of a card-up-front trial ([Quick Sprout](https://www.quicksprout.com/what-converts-better-free-trial-versus-money-back-guarantee/)). That suggests choosing one primary form of risk reversal rather than stacking them. For a done-for-you offer, the most distinctive form is a setup-time guarantee such as "Live in 14 days or your first month is free", stated with plain conditions, because buried conditions undermine trust ([Abmatic](https://abmatic.ai/blog/how-to-use-guarantees-to-increase-conversions-on-landing-page-for-saas)).

## Conversion mechanics favour plain words, a dual CTA, early proof and a mobile-first build

**Readability is the strongest correlate of SaaS conversion in the benchmark data.** Unbounce's 2024 benchmark covered 41,000+ pages and 57M conversions. It puts the **SaaS median conversion rate at 3.8%**, against 6.6% across all industries. SaaS pages written at a 5th–7th grade level converted at **12.9%**, against 2.1% for professional-level copy, and the best length was **250–725 words** ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). These findings are correlational, not tested, but the implication is clear. A page with fifteen sections must keep its *visible* word count low by putting detail in tabs and accordions. The hero should say "Every lead, text, review and booking in one app, set up for you", not "unified omnichannel CRM platform". The same data shows **79% of SaaS landing page visits are mobile**, and mobile converts on par with desktop (6.4% vs 6.2%) ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). NN/g found that 57% of viewing time is spent above the fold, so the value proposition and primary CTA must fit in the first 375px-wide screen ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Traffic source matters too: email traffic converts at a 16.9% median, against roughly 2.9–3.5% for paid social, which should shape budget expectations ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)).

**Pair a self-serve trial with a "book a setup call" calendar, and test whether to require a card.** First Page Sage data, relayed second-hand, shows card-required trials converting to paid at **48.8%** against 18.2% for no-card trials. Visitor-to-trial runs the other way, at 2.5% against 8.5% ([Flint](https://www.flint.com/blog/b2b-saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-statistics)). The right choice depends on whether ib2b values signup volume (cold paid social) or revenue per visitor with limited onboarding capacity, so it belongs in the first A/B test. Whichever wins, the near-universal pattern is two CTAs: a self-serve trial or "Get started", and a sales or demo path. Showing a calendar immediately after the form is supported by vendor cases. Workato's inbound-to-meeting rate rose from roughly 40–55% to 75–80% ([Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/customers/workato)). That fits a service-led offer, provided the team tracks the full path from form to booked call to held call to opportunity, since no-shows can erase the gain. Forms should start short. Textareas and multiple dropdowns depress conversion ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/6746/which-types-of-form-fields-lower-landing-page-conversions.aspx)), and asking for a phone number reportedly costs around 18.7% ([Reform](https://reform.app/blog/single-page-vs-multi-step-forms-which-converts-better)). Phone and SMS consent therefore belong in step two. A sticky mobile CTA lifted conversion 20.4% in one agency test ([Convertibles](https://convertibles.dev/blogs/case-studies/homepage-sticky-cta-case-study)).

**A new brand should borrow proof carefully and place it early.** Moving video testimonials higher lifted conversion from 3.91% to 6.38% in one reported test ([SaaSHero](https://saashero.net/content/b2b-saas-landing-page-examples/)). Testimonials and logos placed directly under the CTA lifted Hubstaff's conversion 59%, from 6.89% to 10.95% ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/blog/social-proof-and-trust-signals-for-conversion-rate-optimization-implementation-and-impact)). Without big logos, the proof order should be: two to four named SMB clients with a specific metric, photo, business name and city; one short video testimonial near the first CTA; a team or founder block answering "who sets this up for you"; and a "your data" block covering hosting, encryption, backups, export and GDPR. Quantified, dated testimonials outperform generic praise ([Peerbound](https://www.peerbound.com/blog/customer-proof-guide)). Platform-scale borrowing ("built on infrastructure used by thousands of businesses") is possible, but must not name GHL or display its review ratings as ib2b's own. Self-serving review markup is also restricted by Google ([Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/google-muzzles-self-serving-review-snippets)).

**Performance is a conversion lever.** In Google's Rakuten 24 A/B test, optimising Core Web Vitals raised conversion rate by 33% ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/rakuten)). The build should meet LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms and CLS < 0.1. That means an optimised static or lightly animated hero visual rather than autoplay video, a click-to-play 60–90 second demo, a lazy-loaded calendar iframe, and deferred chat and heatmap scripts. No controlled test settles hero video versus image ([Common Craft](https://commoncraft.com/node/4251)), so the lighter option is the safer default.

## White-label positioning and compliance set hard limits on the copy

**GHL's Terms permit rebranding but forbid using its marks, misleading claims, and passing off.** The license lets resellers incorporate "your name, logo, trademark". HighLevel marks "may not be used without advance written permission". Resellers must not make "false, misleading or deceptive statements" or represent themselves as HighLevel. They are "fully liable to your customers" and "solely responsible" for disputes, support, 10DLC compliance and HIPAA/PHI handling ([GHL Terms of Service](https://www.gohighlevel.com/terms-of-service)). Exposure is real. Without the paid white-label app, clients download the "LeadConnector" app ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.LeadConnector&hl=en_IN)). Default system emails can show GHL branding until email services are configured ([ghlscaleup](https://www.ghlscaleup.com/blog/gohighlevel-white-label)). Even SMBcrm's cookie banner lists "LeadConnector visitor tracking" ([smbcrm.com](https://smbcrm.com/)). One operator reported losing a client who found "gohighlevel" in page source ([courseplatformsreview](https://www.courseplatformsreview.com/blog/highlevel-saas-mode/)). The defensible stance is quiet transparency. The page says "the ib2b CRM" or "our platform", never claims proprietary or in-house technology, and puts value in the service layer. An FAQ answer such as "built on a proven enterprise platform used by thousands of businesses; we configure, run and support it for you" deals with discovery before a client makes it, with the exact wording reviewed against the ToS.

| Do not say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "GoHighLevel", "HighLevel", "LeadConnector", or show their logos (copy, ads, comparison pages) | ToS trademark clause | "the ib2b CRM", "our platform" |
| "Our proprietary AI, built in-house" | False or misleading statement risk under the ToS | "AI that answers missed calls and books appointments for you" |
| "HIPAA-compliant" | HIPAA sits on GHL's Enterprise tier, and PHI responsibility falls on the reseller | Omit, unless a BAA and Enterprise plan are in place |
| "Start texting today" / "Instant SMS" | A2P 10DLC approval takes days to weeks | "SMS goes live after carrier approval, which we handle for you" |
| "Your own branded mobile app" | Requires the paid white-label app plus store approval, reported at 4–12 weeks | "Mobile app for iOS and Android" |
| Unsourced or inconsistent stats ("12,000+ businesses", "$2.4M recovered") | Credibility damage and FTC substantiation risk | Named client results with dates and a results disclaimer |
| "Unlimited everything" / "No extra fees" | Usage is metered | "Flat plan price; usage included up to X, then published rates" |

**SMS and phone capture make legal plumbing a conversion requirement.** Carriers have blocked unregistered 10DLC traffic since 2023 ([ghlscaleup](https://www.ghlscaleup.com/blog/what-is-a2p-10dlc)). Registration happens per sub-account in GHL's Trust Center, and Low Volume Standard brands pay **$23.95 one-time plus up to $12/month** in campaign fees ([GHL help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001225526-lc-phone-system-trust-center)). Registration is rejected when the site lacks a privacy policy, terms or an opt-in disclosure, or when the support email domain doesn't match ([Signal House](https://signalhouse.io/guide/why-lc-phone-rejects-your-a2p-campaigns/)). ib2b's own landing page is therefore part of its own A2P application. The Eleventh Circuit vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent rule in January 2025 ([Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule)). Prior express written consent is still required for marketing texts ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2025/09/the-fcc-issues-final-rule-formally-eliminating-the-one-to-one-consent-requirement)), and carriers still expect one-to-one-style proof plus a privacy clause saying SMS opt-in data is not shared ([ActiveProspect](https://activeprospect.com/blog/fcc-one-to-one-consent/)). The form needs an unchecked, optional SMS consent box. Its text should say that consent is not a condition of purchase, that message frequency varies, that message and data rates may apply, and how to reply STOP and HELP, with links to the Privacy Policy and SMS Terms. Counsel should confirm the wording.

For EEA and UK traffic, Google Consent Mode v2 has been required since 6 March 2024, behind a consent manager with default-denied states ([Enzuzo](https://www.enzuzo.com/blog/google-consent-mode-requirements-ads)). The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025, with WCAG 2.1 AA as the technical reference. Its scope for B2B services and its microenterprise exemption are nuanced, and overlays do not achieve compliance ([AskEm](https://askem.com/compliance/eaa/)).

## SEO rewards vertical and comparison pages, not "small business CRM" head terms

**The head terms are a bidding war a new domain won't win soon.** US volumes for "best CRM for small business"-type queries sit around **6,600/month at $43–62 CPC**, and "crm software" runs 90,500 ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/best-crm-programs-for-small-business)). HubSpot, Zoho and the review publishers own those SERPs. The realistic targets are low-volume, high-intent clusters: vertical "CRM for [med spa / contractors / real estate / dentists]" pages built from ib2b's niche snapshots; "HubSpot alternative for small business"; feature pages such as missed-call text-back, reviews, booking and WhatsApp; and French and Arabic variants, where competition is likely lower (unverified). "GoHighLevel alternative" draws only about 210 searches/month, and any GHL comparison page would face the trademark constraint above ([LanderLab](https://landerlab.io/blog/8-best-gohighlevel-alternatives)). A single landing page can rank for one intent cluster at most, so the build should be multi-page from the start: home, /pricing, /[vertical], /compare/[competitor] and /features/[feature], with hreflang and RTL support if /fr and /ar launch.

**Structured data has narrowed.** FAQ rich results stopped appearing in Google Search as of May 2026 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results/574429/)). FAQPage markup is harmless and still useful for answer engines, but it earns no SERP space. Use Organization, SoftwareApplication or Product with Offer, and BreadcrumbList JSON-LD ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app)), and mark up only genuine third-party ratings. In Next.js, this means per-page metadata via the Metadata API, canonicals, generated Open Graph images, `sitemap.ts` and `robots.ts`, and server-rendered copy (no client-only text).

## Recommended section-by-section blueprint

The order below follows the converged competitor sequence: hero, proof, features, onboarding, pricing, FAQ, final CTA. Two sections are inserted that the market leaves open: done-for-you launch and the stack calculator. Two CTAs run throughout. The **primary** is "Start free trial" or "Get started" (wording set by the trial decision below). The **secondary** is "Book a free setup call", which opens the embedded calendar. Visible copy should stay near 700–900 words, with everything else in tabs and accordions.

| # | Section | Purpose | Key content | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Sticky header + mobile sticky CTA bar | Keep the next step one tap away | Logo; anchors (Features, Industries, Pricing, FAQ); language switch if FR/AR; on mobile, a bottom bar with the primary CTA and tap-to-call or WhatsApp | Primary (header button); primary + call/WhatsApp (mobile bar) |
| 1 | Hero | State the outcome and offer in 5 seconds | Outcome H1 at a 5th–7th grade level (e.g. "Never miss another lead. We set up your CRM for you."); one-line sub-head naming the bundle (inbox, booking, reviews, follow-up, AI replies); micro-trust row: "Setup included · No contracts · Unlimited users · Live in X days"; optimised product screenshot or muted loop; play button for a 60–90s demo | Primary + secondary |
| 2 | Proof strip | Earn trust right under the CTA | 1 short video testimonial or 3 named client quotes with metrics; real client logos or counts only; no GHL ratings | None (supports the hero CTA) |
| 3 | Cost of inaction | Make the pain concrete | Three pain cards: missed calls (cite 411 Locals 62%), slow response (cite MIT 5 vs 30 min), tool chaos and surprise bills; one-line agitation | Secondary ("See how we fix it in a 15-min call") |

**Build requirements that span the page.** Mobile-first at 375px, with WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 contrast, labelled inputs, keyboard-operable tabs, accordions, toggle and calendar, captions on videos, and `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Meet Core Web Vitals targets using `next/image` and `next/font`, and lazy-load the calendar and video. Load a consent manager wired to Consent Mode v2 before any analytics, with "Reject all" equal to "Accept all". Instrument these GA4 events: `cta_click` (with a location parameter), `pricing_toggle`, `plan_select`, `calculator_interact`, `video_play`, `faq_open`, `form_start`, `form_step_2`, `generate_lead` (key event) and `book_call` (key event). Pass CRM stages back via the Measurement Protocol as `qualify_lead` and `close_convert_lead` ([Google Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267735)). The first A/B tests, in priority order: trial vs "book setup call" as the primary CTA (or card vs no-card), hero readability variants, testimonial video position, and the annual-default toggle. At a 3–4% baseline, only bold changes will reach significance on modest traffic.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what features to show" to "what risk to take off the buyer". Feature parity is guaranteed, because the software is the same GHL that sells for $97 next door. What competitors either charge for (Keap, Vendasta) or leave to the customer (GHL, every self-serve reseller) is the hard part: configuration, A2P registration, deliverability, migration and the first 30 days of training. That work is ib2b's product, and the landing page should treat it as the headline offer rather than a footnote. The same plumbing that limits the copy is also an asset. A clean privacy policy, an SMS consent box, published usage rates and honest, dated proof are prerequisites for ib2b's own carrier registration and ad accounts, and they are also exactly the trust signals the inconsistent reseller pages lack.

The biggest uncertainties are empirical, not strategic. No public data settles trial-versus-demo, card-versus-no-card, or calculator effectiveness for this segment, and the market fork between US/SMS and MENA/WhatsApp changes half the copy. The team should settle the open decisions above, launch the leanest compliant version with real named proof, and let the first A/B test, the primary CTA, decide the funnel rather than competitor convention.
